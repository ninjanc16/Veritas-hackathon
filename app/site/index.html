The solution

Forecast the season. Rank the canals. Fund the worst first.

Three steps, each one built on public data and each one open to inspection inside the platform.

1

Forecast

A random forest model gives each river county a chance of a drought season in the next six months. It learns from the past drought record and makes new predictions from new data: the Drought Monitor each week, reservoirs each day.

2

Rank

Nearly ten thousand canal segments are mapped. Each one with a known soil is scored by how fast that soil leaks times how likely a drought is. Open your district and you see each canal on the map, its soil, its rank and its cost.

3

Fund and test

Enter a budget and a drought severity. The planner lists exactly which canals to seal, what each costs, how long it takes and how much water it saves. The simulation then starts from today's live readings and runs five years forward: doing nothing against acting early.

Honest limits

What this does not claim

A planning tool is only useful if you know where it stops. These are stated on every screen where they matter.

The forecast is a close call, not a crystal ball.
Canals are assumed unlined.No public data says which canals are already lined. Walk a canal before you spend on it.
The price is materials only.Labour, equipment and water quality checks are not in the sealant cost. The sealant has not been field-tested on the Rio Grande.
Nothing here was measured in the field.Water saved is a range worked out from published seepage rates. Texas districts only.
