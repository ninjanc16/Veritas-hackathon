The solution

How RioIQ works

Three steps. All of the data is public, and you can check every step inside the platform.

First

We forecast the drought

A random forest model gives each river county a chance of a drought season in the next six months. It learned from the past drought record, and it gets new data all the time: the Drought Monitor every week, the reservoirs every day.

Then

We rank the canals

We mapped nearly ten thousand canal segments. Each one with a known soil gets a score: how fast that soil leaks, times how likely a drought is. Open your district and you can see every canal, its soil, its rank and its cost.

Last

You set the budget

Type in a budget and how bad a drought to plan for. The planner lists which canals to seal, what each one costs, how long it takes and how much water it saves. A simulation then starts from today's readings and runs five years forward, once doing nothing and once acting early.

Limits

What we don't know

Please read this before you trust a number on this site. The platform repeats each point next to the numbers it affects.

Our forecast is only a little better than a simple rule.
We assumed every canal is unlined.No public data says which canals are already lined. Walk a canal before you spend money on it.
Our price covers materials only.It leaves out labour, equipment and water quality checks. The sealant has not been field-tested on the Rio Grande.
We measured nothing in the field.The water saved is a range worked out from published seepage rates. We cover Texas districts only.
